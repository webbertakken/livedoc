A [Sheet](/help/canvas/plan-tabs/sheets/) formula starts with `=` and can use any of the functions below. They work as they do in Google Sheets and Excel: the same names, the same arguments, the same results. Arguments in `[brackets]` are optional, and `…` means you can give as many as you like.

Typing a function's name in a cell lists the matching functions; inside its brackets a hint shows its arguments, with the one you are on in bold.

## How Formulas Read

- A range is two corners with a colon: `A1:B10`. A whole column is `A:A`, a whole row `3:3`.
- `$` keeps a reference fixed when it is copied or filled: `$A$1` never moves, `A$1` keeps its row, `$A1` its column. **F4** cycles them while you write.
- Another sheet on the tab is named before an `!`, in quotes when its name has spaces: `='Q3 Budget'!B4`.
- A function that returns several values (`SORT`, `FILTER`, `UNIQUE`, `SEQUENCE`) spills into the cells below and to the right. If something is in the way, it shows `#REF!` and names the cell in the way.
- `HYPERLINK` makes a link: `=HYPERLINK("https://docs.nu", "LiveDoc")` shows **LiveDoc** underlined. Hold **Ctrl** (**⌘** on a Mac) and click it to open the page in a new tab, or right-click the cell and choose **Open Link**. A plain click just selects the cell. Only web (`http`, `https`) and email (`mailto`) addresses become links.
- `LET` names a value once and reads it by name: `=LET(avg, AVERAGE(B2:D2), IF(avg>=4, "Great", "Good"))`. `LAMBDA` makes a function of names, called with values in brackets after it: `=LAMBDA(x, x*2)(5)` is 10. `MAP`, `REDUCE`, `SCAN`, `BYROW`, `BYCOL` and `MAKEARRAY` apply one over a range: `=MAP(A1:A5, LAMBDA(x, x*2))` doubles each value.
- `QUERY` runs a query in Google's query language over a range: `=QUERY(A1:C20, "select A, sum(C) where B = 'Eng' group by A order by sum(C) desc", 1)`. Columns are named by their letters (or `Col1`, `Col2`, … over an array), and the header rows become the labels on top.
- `REGEXMATCH`, `REGEXEXTRACT` and `REGEXREPLACE` read patterns as Google Sheets does (RE2): `(?i)` ignores case, `\Q…\E` matches its text as written, and `\s` is a plain space, tab or line break. Lookarounds (`(?=…)`) and backreferences (`\1`) are not supported and show `#REF!`. In a replacement, `$1` puts back the first group and `$0` the whole match: `=REGEXREPLACE(A2, "(\d+)-(\d+)", "$2-$1")` swaps two numbers.
- `SPARKLINE` draws a small chart inside its cell: `=SPARKLINE(B2:M2)` is a line, and options choose another kind and its colours: `=SPARKLINE(B2:M2, {"charttype","column"; "negcolor","red"})`. The kinds are `line`, `column`, `winloss` and `bar`. The cell itself holds no value, so `=IF(A2="", "", SPARKLINE(B2:M2))` draws only where it is wanted.
- Errors tell you what went wrong: `#DIV/0!` divides by zero, `#N/A` found nothing, `#NAME?` is a function name it doesn't know, `#REF!` points at a cell that was deleted, `#VALUE!` got the wrong kind of value, `#NUM!` is a number out of range (or a range too large to read), `#ERROR!` is a formula it can't read. Hover the cell to see why.

## Plan Card Functions

The **Plan Cards** functions read the document's cards, so a sheet can count and add up the work on your boards and stay current as cards move. `=CARDCOUNT("State", "Done")` counts the cards in Done; `=CARDSUM("Estimate", "State", "In Progress")` adds up the estimates of the cards in progress. Fields and values are named as your boards show them.
