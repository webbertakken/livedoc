A pivot table reads a range of a spreadsheet, its first row naming the columns, and writes a summary of it into the cells from one corner. It works as it does in Google Sheets, and it travels in the file: Excel and LibreOffice open it as a pivot table too.

## Making One

1. Click a cell in your data, or select the range.
2. Choose **Insert › Pivot Table**.
3. Check the **Data Range**, then choose **New Sheet** (a sheet called _Pivot Table 1_) or **Existing Sheet** and type the cell it starts at, like `Sheet1!H1`.
4. Click **Create**. The **Pivot Table Editor** opens beside the grid.

## The Pivot Table Editor

- **Rows** and **Columns**: click **Add** and pick a column. Each item of it gets a row (or a column) of its own. Set its **Order**, **Sort By** a value, **Show Totals** and, for rows, **Repeat Row Labels**.
- **Values**: the numbers to summarise. A column of numbers is summed; any other is counted. Change **Summarise By** (SUM, COUNTA, AVERAGE, MAX and more) and **Show As** a percentage of its row, column or the grand total. With values, **Values As** lays them out as columns or rows.
- **Filters**: untick the items to hide; **Select All** and **Clear** tick or untick them all.
- **Suggested**: one click sets up a ready-made summary, such as _SUM of Units for each Region_.
- **Range**: type a new range to read; fields of columns it leaves out go.

Every change shows in the grid at once, and **Undo** takes it back.

## Working With One

- Select a cell of the pivot table and click **Edit** under it to open the editor again.
- Its cells are worked out from the data: typing into them says _You can't edit a pivot table's cells_. Change the data instead, and the pivot table follows.
- To remove it, select all of its cells and press **Delete**.
- Formulas can read it like any cell, or by name with `GETPIVOTDATA`.

## Who Can Do What

Editors make and change pivot tables. Participants and viewers see them, but not the **Edit** button or **Insert › Pivot Table**.
